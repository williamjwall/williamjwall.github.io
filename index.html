I study linguistics and computing at UCLA. Mostly I think about meaning — how a sentence ends up saying more than its words do on their own, and whether a machine can learn to put meaning together the way we seem to.

Before this, I spent a while in data engineering, most recently as a contractor at returnPro working on supply-chain software. Before that I was a teaching assistant for the data science bootcamp at Le Wagon in Mexico City. I live in Los Angeles. At UCLA I also do Brazilian jiu-jitsu.

Things I've been exploring

  • Tree-LSTMs and compositional semantics (2025)
    side project  |  code on GitHub Language has structure: words make phrases, phrases make clauses. Most neural nets read a sentence left to right and ignore that. A Tree-LSTM follows the parse tree instead, building up the meaning of each phrase from its parts. I wanted to see what that actually looks like, so I wired up a parser, BERT embeddings, and a Child-Sum Tree-LSTM and started poking at it.
